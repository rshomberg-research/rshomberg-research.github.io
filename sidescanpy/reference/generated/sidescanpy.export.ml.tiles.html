sidescanpy.export.ml.tiles#

Swath tiles for annotation and detection, and boxes drawn on them back to targets.

The round trip a detector workflow needs:

  1. write_swath_tiles() cuts a channel into dual-swath tiles (swath_tiles()), writes each as an 8-bit PNG and records where every tile sits in the sonar data in an index file (tiles.json).

  2. People annotate the tiles, or a detector predicts boxes on them, in any tool that exports COCO (FiftyOne, CVAT, Label Studio, …). read_coco_boxes() reads the boxes per image.

  3. boxes_to_targets() turns the boxes on one tile into Target records: file, ping, side, sample and, on a georeferenced channel, latitude and longitude. They can be rendered as chips with build_target_dataset() or scored against a catalog with sidescanpy.targets.score.match_targets().

sidescanpy.export.ml.tiles.boxes_to_targets(ds, record, boxes, *, source_format='tile_box', catalog_source='')#

Turn boxes drawn on one tile into targets in the sonar data.

Each box becomes a Target at the box centre: the side under the centre (a dual-swath box crossing nadir is clipped to that side), the ping and sample there, and, when ds is georeferenced (latitude_per_sample), the position of that sample.

Parameters:
dsxr.Dataset

The channel the tile was cut from (record.ss_h5, record.freq_key), with the same pings and samples.

recordTileRecord

The tile (from write_swath_tiles() or read_tile_index()).

boxesiterable of TileBox
source_format, catalog_sourcestr

Recorded on each target (e.g. "detector" and the model path).

Returns:
list of Target

ping and sample are the dataset’s ping and sample coordinates at the box centre (the axes a catalog’s ping and sample refer to). extra_attributes holds ss_h5 and ping_index (so correlate_target() places the target on that exact ping), ping_meters_per_sample, score, tile and the box size in metres (length_m along track, width_m across). position_confidence is "verified" for a box without a score (a person drew it) and "approximate" for a detection.
